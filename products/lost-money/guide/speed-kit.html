How to Use This Kit (15 minutes)

Most people search the state they live in now. That misses money from every other state in their life. Use the 3-layer plan:

LayerSearch these statesWhy
1. Your lifeEvery state you've lived, worked, gone to school or banked inPaychecks, deposits and accounts are reported to the state of your last known address
2. Company headquartersStates where your past employers, banks, insurers or brokers were headquartered (Delaware is common)When a company has no address for you, it usually reports to its state of incorporation
3. Your familyEvery state where deceased parents, grandparents or a spouse livedHeirs can claim, and this is where large forgotten amounts often sit
Name rule: in each state, search your full name, your last name only (if uncommon), your maiden/previous names and common misspellings. Some states list only "over $100", so claim anyway.

Claim Document Checklist

If you are claiming…Usually have ready
Your own propertyPhoto ID · Social Security number · proof of the address shown on the record (old bill, lease, pay stub, tax return)
Property under a previous nameAll of the above + marriage certificate, divorce decree or name-change document
A business's propertyProof you're authorized (officer/owner), EIN, business documents
A deceased relative's propertyDeath certificate · proof of relationship · estate documents or the state's small-estate affidavit (see the Family Heir Search Kit)
Faster payment: claim all records in one state together when the site allows it, upload clear scans, use the same name/address as on the record, and note your claim number. Check status every 2–3 weeks.

Your Re-Check Calendar

Companies report new unclaimed money to states every year, so a search that finds nothing today can find something next year.
