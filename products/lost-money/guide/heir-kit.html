The Family Heir Search Kit

How to find, and claim, money that a parent, grandparent or spouse left behind and nobody ever collected.

Educational guide. Not legal advice. Not affiliated with any government agency. Searching and claiming through official programs is free.

Why Family Money Goes Unclaimed

When someone passes away, families usually handle the obvious: the house, the main bank account, the car. But small and forgotten accounts are easy to miss: an old savings account, a final paycheck, a utility deposit, dividends from stock bought decades ago, a small life insurance policy from a former job, savings bonds in a drawer.

When the company can't reach the owner, the money is eventually turned over to the state, where it can wait for years. Heirs can claim it. But only if someone in the family goes looking.

One real example: an Ohio family recovered savings bonds, cash and coins from their late parents' safe deposit box that had been turned over to the state (reported by FOX 8 Cleveland, 2025).

Who should use this kit

  • Anyone who lost a parent, grandparent, spouse, sibling or other relative, even many years ago.
  • The executor or administrator of an estate.
  • Anyone who suspects a relative "had money somewhere" but never found it.

Step 2: Find the Clues in Their Paperwork

Old paperwork is a treasure map. Look for:

ClueWhat it may point to
Tax returns and 1099 forms (1099-INT, 1099-DIV)Bank accounts, brokerage accounts and stocks paying interest or dividends
Bank statements with automatic paymentsInsurance premiums (= a policy exists), other accounts
Pay stubs, retirement letters, union cardsPensions, 401(k)s, group life insurance from employers
Stock certificates, dividend checks, company newslettersShares that may now be held by a state
Paper savings bonds, or notes like "bonds for the kids"U.S. savings bonds (replaceable even if lost)
Keys to a safe deposit boxBox contents. If rent went unpaid, contents may have been sent to the state
Mail arriving after deathAccounts, policies and refunds you didn't know about
Tip: Don't throw away mail that keeps arriving for a deceased relative for at least a year. It often reveals accounts nobody knew about.

Step 3: The Heir Search Circuit

Search each of these for every name variation of your relative:

  1. MissingMoney.com: multi-state search.
  2. Each state's own program for every state they lived or worked in (links in the 50-State list on unclaimed.org, or in the 50-State Speed Kit).
  3. NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator: free request; insurers contact you if you're a beneficiary.
  4. TreasuryDirect.gov: matured or lost savings bonds (lost bonds: FS Form 1048).
  5. PBGC unclaimed pensions (pbgc.gov), if they worked for a company whose pension plan ended.
  6. VA unclaimed insurance funds (va.gov), if they were a veteran.
  7. Former employers' HR / benefits departments: ask about final pay, pensions and group life insurance (letter template in this kit).
Watch for scams: official programs never charge to search or claim. Never pay a "release fee".

Step 4: The Documents Heirs Usually Need

Requirements are set by each state and each company, so always read the instructions on the official claim page. In most cases, expect some combination of:

DocumentWhere to get it
Certified death certificateThe vital records office of the state where the death occurred. The CDC's "Where to Write for Vital Records" page lists every state's office.
Proof of your identityYour photo ID; sometimes your Social Security number
Proof of relationshipBirth certificates (yours, showing the parent), marriage certificate (for spouses)
Proof of authority over the estateIf an estate was opened: court documents naming the executor/administrator (often called letters testamentary or letters of administration)
Small-estate affidavit (if no estate was opened)Many states allow heirs to claim smaller amounts with a sworn affidavit instead of probate. Limits and forms vary by state, so check the state's claim instructions or call the program
Proof of the relative's address on the recordOld bills, tax returns, letters showing the address listed in the database
Siblings & multiple heirs: when several heirs exist, states often require all of them to sign, or one to be authorized by the others or by the estate. Agree as a family before filing to avoid delays.

Letter Templates

A. To a bank or credit union (accounts of a deceased relative)

[Date] [Bank name] · Attn: Estate Services / Deceased Accounts [Address] Re: Accounts of [Full name], date of death [date], SSN ending [last 4] I am the [relationship / executor] of [full name], who passed away on [date]. Please search your records for any open, closed, dormant or escheated accounts, certificates of deposit or safe deposit boxes in this name. If any funds were reported to a state as unclaimed property, please tell me which state and the date reported. Enclosed: certified death certificate and proof of my relationship/authority. Contact: [Address] | [Phone] | [Email] Sincerely, [Name]

B. To a former employer (pension, final pay, group life insurance)

[Date] [Company] · Attn: Human Resources / Benefits Re: Benefits of former employee [Full name], employed approx. [years], DOB [date], date of death [date] I am writing as the [relationship / executor] of [full name], a former employee. Please confirm whether any of the following exist or were unpaid: final wages, pension or retirement plan balance, group life insurance, or other benefits. If the plan was transferred or terminated, please tell me the plan administrator's name. Enclosed: death certificate and proof of my relationship/authority. Contact: [Address] | [Phone] | [Email] Thank you, [Name]

C. Cover letter for a state heir claim

[Date] [State] Unclaimed Property Program Re: Heir claim · Owner: [Deceased's full name] · Property ID(s): [numbers] I am filing a claim as [heir / executor] of the owner, who passed away on [date]. Enclosed are the completed claim form and the documents listed below: 1. Certified death certificate 2. My photo ID 3. Proof of relationship: [list] 4. [Estate documents OR small-estate affidavit] 5. Proof of the owner's address shown on the record: [document] Please contact me if anything else is needed. [Name] | [Phone] | [Email]
