## EMAIL 2: Day 1

**Subject:** The name trick most people miss
**Alt subject:** Did you search for "Bob" or "Robert"?
**Preview text:** A misspelled record won't show up under your real name.

---

{{first_name}},

Quick question about yesterday's search.

Did you search under **one name**? Or every version of it?

Here's the thing most people miss:

**Unclaimed property is listed exactly as the company typed it.**

Not as your name actually is. As a clerk, a payroll system, or a bank form typed it. Maybe 25 years ago. Maybe in a hurry.

So the record could be under:

- **A nickname:** Bob instead of Robert, Peggy instead of Margaret, Bill instead of William
- **Initials:** "J. Smith" or "J.R. Smith" instead of "James Smith"
- **A maiden name** or a previous married name
- **A typo:** Jonhson, Mcdonald vs. McDonald, a missing letter
- **A business name:** a side business, a small shop, a sole proprietorship
- **A joint account:** listed under your spouse's name first

---

**Try this today (10 minutes):**

1. Open **Chapter 2** of your Playbook (the name variations step): {{playbook_url}}
2. Write every version of your name on your Search Tracker.
3. Search the last name only, then scan the results for your old addresses and cities.
4. Repeat in every state you've lived in.

That last tip matters. Searching **last name only** shows you more results. Then you look for a city or address you recognize.

It takes a few extra minutes. But if a record was typed wrong, this is the only way you'll find it.
